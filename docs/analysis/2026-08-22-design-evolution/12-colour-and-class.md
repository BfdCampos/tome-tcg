# Colour × Class — the two-axis archetype system

The correction that reshaped deckbuilding. **Colour is not deck identity.** An
earlier draft (doc 05, since fixed) wrongly suggested "two-colour decks" like MTG —
that's wrong for an RPS game. Here's the right model.

## Two independent axes

Every spell has **both**:

### Axis 1 — Colour (🔴🟢🔵): your RPS identity

- The **only** thing the clash cares about (Red > Green > Blue > Red).
- **You cannot refuse to be able to throw "rock."** A deck that can't attack in some
  colour is just RPS-exploitable — the opponent parks on the colour you can't answer.
- Therefore **most decks run all three colours.** A deck may **skew** (a fire deck is
  red-heavy) but should keep real options in green and blue for coverage.

### Axis 2 — Class: your archetype & synergy

- A **class/type** on each card (Pyromancer, Warlock, Sorcerer, Druid, … — final list
  TBD) that themes the deck and is where **synergy** lives.
- **A class spans all three colours**, reflavoured to its theme. The class is the
  *concept*; the colours are how that concept covers the wheel.
- This is the deckbuilding axis: you build **around a class**, not around a colour.

## The reflavour principle (why all-colour decks still feel coherent)

A class gives its off-colours a **thematic reskin** so a three-colour deck reads as
one idea, not a rainbow pile. The fire example the design brief gave:

| Colour in a **Pyromancer** deck | Reflavour | e.g. |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 Red (primary) | the fire itself | *Fireball*, *Flaming Sword* |
| 🟢 Green (secondary) | **kindling / fuel** | *Wildfire*, *Dry Brush* |
| 🔵 Blue (splash) | **oil / steam** | *Oil Slick*, *Scalding Steam* |

Same wheel coverage, one coherent fantasy. A Tide class would reflavour red as
*warm currents* or *geysers*; a Druid class would reflavour blue as *rain* and red as
*wildfire*; etc.

## Should class be printed, or just flavour?

**Decision: class is a real, printed *type* (an icon/label on the card), and some
effects care about it.** Reasons:

- It's the **synergy hook** — *"your Pyromancer spells get +2 Hit damage after you
  complete a quest"* only works if class is a mechanical tag.
- It's the **deckbuilding signpost** — players collect and build along class lines
  (the RPG "which school of magic am I" fantasy).
- It doubles as a **collection/lore axis** for the economy (doc 07) and the world
  (doc 06): classes map to schools/factions.

So: **Colour** (the RPS tag) and **Class** (the archetype tag) are both first-class
card fields.

## What deckbuilding tension now looks like

The "what's the strat" question, answered. A deck is:

> **a class + a colour spread + a role mix + a matched 7-quest line.**

Illustrative **13-spell** Pyromancer build:
- **Colour spread** — ~7 🔴 / ~4 🟢 / ~2 🔵 (skewed to red, but never blind in a
  colour).
- **Role mix** — ~4 **hitters** (big modifier), ~5 **situational** (Seals,
  colour-denial, conditional), ~2 **defensive** (`Lose:` Shield/Heal), ~2 **enablers**
  (`Learn:` draw, ramp synergy).
- **7 quests** — themed to reward the class's plan, with 1–2 loser-completable
  comeback tasks and "OR" escape hatches (doc 10).

The tension: you **can't** jam 13 hitters (no answers, and you'd be readable), you
**can't** go mono-colour (RPS-exploitable), and your quests must fit your colours or
you'll get colour-denied into a dead questline. Every choice costs something — which
is the point.

## Open sub-questions

- **The class list** — how many classes at launch, and their names/identities? (Ties
  into world/naming, doc 06, and sets, doc 07.)
- **How heavy is class synergy?** Light (a few payoff cards) or heavy (most cards want
  a class)? Leaning **light-to-medium** at launch so colour/RPS stays the star and
  classes don't become a second full rules layer.
- **Neutral/classless cards?** Probably yes — flexible "glue" spells any class can
  run, mirroring the neutral cards the `2026-08-17` review recommended.
